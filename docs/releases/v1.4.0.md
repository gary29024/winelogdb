# WineLogDB v1.4.0

**Release date:** 2026-10-06

WineLogDB 1.4 is the release where the app grows from a multi-user wine journal with reference identity into a broader **wine-exploration and research platform**.

The two largest additions are a much deeper Burgundy geography/parcel layer and the new **Vintages** experience. Alongside those, Deep Search is substantially more durable, member access is easier, research can be read in Traditional Chinese, and the operational controls around AI/provider uncertainty have been tightened.

This release consolidates 87 merged product PRs after v1.3.0. It deliberately excludes still-open PR #472.

## Highlights

### Burgundy maps now cover the region as an in-app geographic layer

v1.3 introduced verified Burgundy Atlas links. v1.4 goes much further by bringing Burgundy geography into WineLog itself.

The map rollout now covers the main village and regional surfaces across:

- Côte de Nuits;
- Côte de Beaune;
- Côte Chalonnaise;
- Mâconnais;
- Chablis and the Grand Auxerrois;
- broad Bourgogne regional appellations where the source geography can be represented responsibly.

Maps support cru/appellation highlighting and scoped denomination matching. Region-wide buttons are deliberately withheld where a single map would imply a precision the underlying appellation does not have.

The implementation was also made practical for a free-tier-oriented deployment: large geometries are compressed losslessly, map assets are pinned, and Chromium regression coverage is tiered around distinct behaviours rather than repeating the same browser journey for every map.

### Grand Cru parcel research becomes a reusable evidence system

The Échezeaux pilot grew into a reusable Grand Cru parcel pipeline rather than remaining a one-off map.

The parcel layer can now combine, where evidence exists:

- cadastral parcels and named areas;
- current holder/company-register context;
- dated rights-history lineage;
- farm-structure notices;
- sale/deed evidence;
- older official parcel references;
- curated producer/farming evidence.

Manual producer links are account-scoped and explicitly labelled when farming is unverified. They do not rewrite cadastral rights, wine identity or verified operator assignments.

Official historical parcel evidence is now indexed across all 33 Burgundy Grand Crus. That does **not** mean WineLog claims every current owner/operator is fully resolved: uncertain holder, lease and farming relationships remain visibly unresolved rather than being inferred into a clean-looking answer.

### Vintages replaces Insights with a season-first wine view

The previous Insights tab is replaced by a dedicated **Vintages** tab.

Instead of summarising only what has been logged, Vintages asks a different question: *what happened in this growing season, and what does that imply for the wine?*

For Burgundy, the model was rebuilt and checked against published/observed records. The release includes:

- flowering and véraison timing based on published phenology modelling and observed calibration;
- village-relative warmth, rainfall and night-temperature grading rather than arbitrary fixed bands;
- daily sunshine treatment during ripening;
- a four-driver ripening card;
- grape-aware season narratives;
- BIVB measured must-sugar anchors where available;
- a calibrated quality outlook and explicit accuracy limitations;
- the 2026 season.

Vintages then expands outside Burgundy:

- Bordeaux red coverage by major communes/banks, including grape and blend views;
- Pessac-Léognan dry whites;
- Sauternes and Barsac;
- sourced Bordeaux sugar/harvest context where available;
- Champagne across four sub-regions with UMC must data and critics’ outlooks.

A scheduled vintage-data workflow maintains the underlying source-data build outside the interactive app path.

### Deep Search is safer when providers, networks or evidence fail

Research now has a much more explicit durability model.

Member Deep Search can recover accepted work after browser/network interruption, replay saved provider replies without buying the same work again, and reconcile uncertain provider outcomes through bounded status checks.

When a provider outcome is genuinely uncertain, the UI can show that state instead of pretending the request simply failed. A user can **Check status**; eligible held work can be closed with **Stop waiting**; the owner can release stuck operations from Owner Controls with a warning that a provider call which actually completed could otherwise be paid for twice.

Partial validated research is preserved when another scope fails. WineLog also stops expensive ungrounded retries where grounded evidence is the requirement.

Deep Search’s evidence rules were refined as well:

- an uncited precise figure is labelled instead of invalidating the entire report;
- vintage research is kept separate by wine style;
- older pre-style vintage reports can still be reused when the current style is unambiguous;
- the Journal can mark/filter wines with complete Deep Search;
- a member’s own completed research on a shared bottle is tracked for that member rather than incorrectly depending on the source owner’s flag.

### Non-vintage wines are researched by release, not just by cuvée

Non-vintage Champagne and other release-driven wines no longer have to share one generic exact-wine research identity.

When available, WineLog now includes release designation, base year and disgorgement in the exact-wine research identity. That allows, for example, two different editions of the same cuvée to remain distinct while still reusing safe producer/terroir context.

Existing generic research is retained as a prior fallback until a release-specific refresh is performed. Vintage wines and non-vintage wines with no release information keep their prior behaviour.

### Shared research and producer ranges are preserved more carefully

Research and producer-range reuse across friends was tightened so the useful shared context survives without weakening account boundaries.

A recipient can reuse eligible friend-shared research and visible producer-range context, while private source-owner experience stays outside the recipient’s research/search document. Producer catalogue links are now built from the range actually being shown, including a visible friend/LWIN range, instead of assuming the current account already owns a duplicate range.

### Accounts are easier to join and easier to identify

Accounts now have unique `@handles`. Handles can be used to add friends and can change without breaking friendships, shares or tastings because the stable account ID remains the real relationship key.

WineLog can also run in capacity-controlled open-sign-up mode: any Google account may join while the deployment has room. This removes the need to pre-create every member invitation while retaining the deployment’s capacity/budget gate.

`Account & friends`, `Owner controls` and the account entry point received another mobile-focused redesign, including clearer storage/cost reporting and maintenance actions.

Owner AI operations are no longer blocked by member admission limits. They still retain usage accounting and duplicate-work safeguards. Member failures now identify the applicable capacity/budget reason more clearly.

Cloudflare-cost accounting also rolls forward with each new billing month rather than unnecessarily pausing members at the month boundary.

### Deep Search and producer research can be read in Traditional Chinese

Wine and producer research now has an **EN / 中** switch using Traditional Chinese wording aimed at Hong Kong readers. The Deep Search header keeps the research-status badge at the end of the title row and the compact language switch on the row below beside the quality score, preserving the intended mobile hierarchy.

The translation system is deliberately narrower than full application localization. It translates research content and the immediate research controls while keeping producer/wine names and useful wine terminology in English where that improves clarity.

Translations are stored per research section against a hash of the exact English plus prompt version. This matters because a displayed Deep Search result is assembled from reusable producer, terroir and vintage scopes: a translated section can therefore be reused anywhere that exact research appears, including shared research, without translating a whole assembled report again.

New Deep Search and producer-research runs attempt translation as a best-effort final step. Translation failure does not fail or hold an otherwise completed research run. Older research can be backfilled by the owner, using Vertex Flex where it is actually served.

Usage accounting records translation as a separate step within the originating research run. The served tier is taken from provider usage metadata, so a Flex request that was served at standard tier is costed as standard instead of being understated.

### CI and payload work keep the larger app affordable to operate

The feature set grew substantially, especially the map/research datasets, so v1.4 also includes explicit cost controls around development and delivery.

- Burgundy browser tests are organised around distinct behaviours instead of duplicating the same checks for every map.
- Draft PRs can defer expensive browser suites until the work is ready for review.
- Merged PRs are not immediately re-tested a second time on `main` when the tested PR head is the same product change.
- Large map geometries are compressed without changing their boundaries.
- Vintage data refresh runs as its own scheduled build path.

These changes keep regression coverage while reducing GitHub Actions and runtime/storage pressure.

For a self-hosted deployment, back up D1/R2 as appropriate and use the repository-supported command:

```bash
npm run deploy
```

That command builds the application, applies outstanding **remote D1 migrations**, and only then deploys the Worker. Do not replace it with a bare `wrangler deploy` for this upgrade.

No LWIN reference re-import is required merely because the application version changes. Existing current R2 reference catalogues remain separate operational data.

## Notes worth knowing

- **Vintages replaces Insights.** This is an intentional navigation/product change, not a migration of historical Insights charts.
- **Parcel evidence is evidence, not a guessed ownership graph.** Current owner/operator/farming relationships remain unresolved when the public records do not establish them.
- **Manual parcel-producer links are scoped and explicit.** They do not rewrite cadastral rights or WineLog wine identity.
- **Research translation is not full UI localization.** The new language switch applies to Deep Search/producer-research content and its immediate controls.
- **Open sign-up remains capacity-controlled.** A Google account can join only while the deployment’s configured room/budget gate allows it.
- **Held provider work is intentionally conservative.** WineLog prefers a visible uncertain state over silently paying for a duplicate provider call.
- **Shared research remains privacy-scoped.** Reuse does not expose the source owner’s private tasting experience.
